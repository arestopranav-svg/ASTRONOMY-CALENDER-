Supermoons 2026

The biggest and brightest full moons of the year

Supermoons occur when a full moon coincides with the Moon's closest approach to Earth (perigee). In 2026, we can expect several supermoons that will appear larger and brighter than average full moons.

2026 Supermoon Dates

  • January 14 - Wolf Moon Supermoon
  • February 12 - Snow Moon Supermoon
  • March 14 - Worm Moon Supermoon
  • April 13 - Pink Moon Supermoon
  • May 12 - Flower Moon Supermoon
  • June 11 - Strawberry Moon Supermoon
  • July 10 - Buck Moon Supermoon
  • August 9 - Sturgeon Moon Supermoon
  • September 7 - Harvest Moon Supermoon
  • October 7 - Hunter's Moon Supermoon
  • November 5 - Beaver Moon Supermoon
  • December 5 - Cold Moon Supermoon

What Makes a Supermoon Special?

A supermoon can appear up to 14% larger and 30% brighter than a typical full moon. This happens because the Moon's orbit is elliptical, and when the full moon occurs near perigee, it appears closer and larger.

Asteroids

Near-Earth asteroid approaches and observations

Notable Asteroid Events in 2026

Several near-Earth asteroids will make close approaches in 2026. While none pose a threat to Earth, they provide excellent opportunities for scientific observation and amateur astronomy.

Key Asteroids to Watch

  • 3200 Phaethon - Parent body of the Geminids meteor shower. Best observed in December.
  • 4 Vesta - The brightest asteroid, visible in binoculars during opposition.
  • 1 Ceres - The largest object in the asteroid belt, classified as a dwarf planet.
  • 2 Pallas - Second asteroid discovered, visible with small telescopes.

Asteroid Observation Tips

Asteroids appear as star-like points of light that move slowly against the background stars. To observe them, you'll need a star chart showing their position and a telescope or binoculars.

Deep Sky Events

Galaxies, nebulae, and star clusters to observe

Best Deep Sky Objects for 2026

The best time to observe deep sky objects is during new moon periods when the sky is darkest. Here are the top targets for 2026:

Winter Targets (Dec-Feb)

  • Orion Nebula (M42) - The closest massive star-forming region to Earth
  • Andromeda Galaxy (M31) - Our nearest major galaxy neighbor
  • Pleiades (M45) - Beautiful open star cluster
  • Crab Nebula (M1) - Supernova remnant in Taurus

Spring Targets (Mar-May)

  • Whirlpool Galaxy (M51) - Famous interacting galaxy pair
  • Sombrero Galaxy (M104) - Edge-on spiral galaxy with dark dust lane
  • Virgo Galaxy Cluster - Rich cluster of galaxies

Summer Targets (Jun-Aug)

  • Hercules Globular Cluster (M13) - Brightest globular cluster in the northern sky
  • Ring Nebula (M57) - Planetary nebula in Lyra
  • Dumbbell Nebula (M27) - First planetary nebula discovered

Autumn Targets (Sep-Nov)

  • Triangulum Galaxy (M33) - Third-largest galaxy in the Local Group
  • Double Cluster (NGC 869/884) - Pair of bright open clusters
  • Andromeda Galaxy (M31) - Best viewed in autumn evenings

Track the International Space Station from your location

How to See the ISS

The International Space Station is visible to the naked eye as a bright, fast-moving star. It orbits Earth every 90 minutes, completing about 16 orbits per day.

Tips for ISS Viewing

  • The ISS is brightest when it passes overhead during twilight hours
  • It appears as a steady, bright white light moving across the sky
  • Passes typically last 2-4 minutes
  • Use apps like ISS Detector or NASA's Spot The Station for precise timings
  • Best viewing is when the ISS is at least 40 degrees above the horizon

What You'll See

The ISS appears as a bright, steady point of light moving smoothly across the sky. It doesn't flash like an airplane - its solar panels reflect sunlight, giving it a constant brightness. At its brightest, it can rival Venus.

2026 Notable ISS Events

  • Regular crew rotations and spacewalks throughout the year
  • Continued construction of new modules
  • Scientific experiments and technology demonstrations

How to Use Sky Maps

Sky maps help you navigate the night sky and locate celestial objects. Here are some recommended tools:

Recommended Apps & Websites

  • Stellarium - Free, open-source planetarium software
  • SkySafari - Comprehensive mobile app for iOS and Android
  • Star Walk 2 - Beautiful augmented reality star gazing app
  • Heavens-Above - Satellite and ISS tracking
  • TimeAndDate.com - Night sky maps for any location

Reading a Star Chart

Star charts show the sky as seen from your location at a specific time. The center of the chart represents the zenith (directly overhead), and the edges represent the horizon. North is typically at the top.

Beginner's Astronomy

Your guide to exploring the night sky

Getting Started

Stargazing is one of the most accessible hobbies. You don't need expensive equipment to begin - just your eyes and a dark sky.

Essential Equipment

  • Red flashlight - Preserves your night vision
  • Star chart or app - Helps you identify constellations
  • Warm clothing - Nights can get cold, even in summer
  • Reclining chair or blanket - For comfortable meteor watching
  • Binoculars (7x50 or 10x50) - Great first instrument for deep sky objects

First Targets

  • The Moon - Best viewed during partial phases for crater detail
  • Jupiter and its moons - Visible in binoculars
  • Saturn's rings - Visible in even small telescopes
  • Orion Nebula (M42) - Visible in binoculars from dark skies
  • Andromeda Galaxy (M31) - Visible to naked eye from dark locations

Dark Sky Tips

Light pollution is the biggest enemy of stargazing. Find a dark location away from city lights. Allow 20-30 minutes for your eyes to fully adapt to the dark. Avoid looking at your phone!
